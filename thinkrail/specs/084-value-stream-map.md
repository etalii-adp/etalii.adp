## Context

**The problem.** In IT service management as ITIL 4 frames it, a value stream is the series of steps an organisation takes to create one outcome for a consumer, such as restoring a service or fulfilling a request. A value stream draws on several practices at once: restoring a service touches incident management, monitoring, problem management, change enablement and configuration management. Mapping how a stream actually runs, with its waiting and its hand-offs, is the central analytical act of the framework's continual improvement. Practitioners still do it with paper and sticky notes, or on generic whiteboards that know nothing of practices, hand-offs or waiting time. Workflow suites model processes as executable workflows, which say what is supposed to happen rather than what does. The result is a picture that cannot be measured, compared or kept beside the improvement it motivated.

**What this feature delivers.** The definition of a new diagram type, the **Value stream map** (`generic/value-stream-map`), in this repository: its specification, its document format and binding, its tool description, its companion document and worked examples, so that every host can build the same diagram from them. Building it in a host is a separate feature per host and is not in scope.

**The framework's versions.** ITIL (Version 5), launched in February 2026, keeps the practices and replaces the six activities of the service value chain (plan, improve, engage, design and transition, obtain/build, deliver and support) with an eight-activity product and service lifecycle (discover, design, acquire, build, transition, operate, deliver, support). A value stream map built on only one of these lists would age with it. The diagram therefore holds the activity vocabulary in the document, starting from either list, and is not bound to a framework version.

**Naming.** ITIL is a registered trademark, and its publications are not freely licensed. The diagram is named for the concept, not the framework, and carries no text taken from the publications. Practice names are used only as short labels a user can change.

**Words.** A **value stream** is one path from a trigger (a demand or an event) to an outcome (value for a consumer). A **step** is one unit of work in it. A **practice** is an organisational capability a step draws on. An **activity** is one entry of the activity vocabulary, which classifies a step. A **hand-off** is a passage of work from one step to the next where the performer changes. **Process time** is the time a step is actively worked on; **wait time** is the time work waits before it. **Lead time** is the sum of both over the stream; **flow efficiency** is total process time divided by lead time. A **state** of a map is *current* (as it runs) or *future* (as intended). Other words are those of [docs/terminology.md](../../docs/terminology.md).

### What this feature is not

- It is not a process engine: nothing is executed, routed or assigned.
- It is not a business process notation: a step has no gateways, events or message flows. Branching is limited to the alternatives of User Story 4.
- It is not a measurement system: times are entered or estimated by people or written by agents, not collected from a ticketing system.

## User Scenarios & Testing *(mandatory)*

### User Story 1 - Draw a value stream as it runs (Priority: P1)

A service owner maps how a "restore service" stream actually runs. They place the trigger, the steps in order and the outcome, name each step, set its activity and the practices it draws on, and enter its process time and wait time. The diagram draws the steps left to right with the trigger at the start and the outcome at the end, waiting time as a band below the steps, and each hand-off as a marked connection.

**Why this priority**: this is the mapping exercise itself. With nothing else built, it replaces the wall of sticky notes with a picture that is a file.

**Independent Test**: draw a stream of six steps with times, close the document, open it again, and compare the drawing and the file.

**Acceptance Scenarios**:

1. **Given** an empty value stream map, **When** the user adds a trigger, four steps and an outcome and connects them, **Then** they are drawn left to right in flow order, with the trigger first and the outcome last.
2. **Given** a step with a process time of 20 minutes and a wait time of 3 hours, **When** the map is shown, **Then** the step shows its process time and the band below it shows its wait time, in the unit the user entered.
3. **Given** two connected steps whose performers differ, **When** the map is shown, **Then** the connection between them is drawn as a hand-off.
4. **Given** a step that draws on two practices, **When** it is shown, **Then** both practices appear on it as labels.

---

### User Story 2 - See where the stream loses time (Priority: P1)

The service owner wants to know where improvement pays. The diagram computes the stream's lead time, total process time, flow efficiency and number of hand-offs, shows them in a summary, and marks the steps with the longest waits.

**Why this priority**: measuring the stream is what makes a map more than a drawing, and it is why the exercise is done.

**Independent Test**: build a stream from a worked example with known totals and compare every figure the diagram shows with the hand-calculated value.

**Acceptance Scenarios**:

1. **Given** a stream whose steps have process times 10, 20 and 30 minutes and wait times 0, 60 and 120 minutes, **When** the summary is shown, **Then** lead time is 240 minutes, process time 60 minutes and flow efficiency 25 %.
2. **Given** a stream with five connections of which three are hand-offs, **When** the summary is shown, **Then** it reports three hand-offs.
3. **Given** a stream, **When** it is shown, **Then** the steps whose wait time is among the three longest are marked, and the mark names the wait.
4. **Given** a step without a wait time, **When** totals are computed, **Then** the step counts as zero and a finding says that its wait time is missing.

---

### User Story 3 - Compare the current and the future stream (Priority: P2)

An improvement team designs the stream as it should run. In the same document they keep the current stream and a future one, and see the difference in lead time, flow efficiency and hand-offs between the two.

**Why this priority**: continual improvement is the reason streams are mapped; a future state with nothing to compare against is a guess.

**Independent Test**: from a current stream, create a future state, remove one hand-off and shorten one wait, and check the differences the diagram reports.

**Acceptance Scenarios**:

1. **Given** a current stream, **When** the user creates a future state from it, **Then** the future state starts as a copy and both are kept in one document.
2. **Given** both states, **When** the user switches between them, **Then** the diagram shows one at a time and says which one is shown.
3. **Given** both states, **When** the comparison is shown, **Then** it gives lead time, flow efficiency and hand-off count for each and the difference between them.

---

### User Story 4 - Alternatives and rework (Priority: P2)

Not every stream is a straight line. A request may be fulfilled by a standard path or escalated, and a failed check sends work back. The user marks a connection as an alternative with its share of the work, or as rework back to an earlier step, and the totals take both into account.

**Why this priority**: rework and escalation are where streams usually lose most time, and a straight-line map hides them.

**Independent Test**: build a stream with one alternative split 80/20 and one rework loop at 10 %, and compare the totals with the worked example's.

**Acceptance Scenarios**:

1. **Given** a step with two outgoing alternatives at 80 % and 20 %, **When** totals are computed, **Then** each path's times are weighted by its share.
2. **Given** a rework connection back to an earlier step with a rate of 10 %, **When** totals are computed, **Then** the repeated steps are added in proportion to the rate.
3. **Given** a step whose outgoing shares do not sum to 100 %, **When** the map is shown, **Then** a finding stands at the step.

---

### User Story 5 - A person and an agent work on the same map (Priority: P2)

An agent reads the document as text, for example to summarise where a stream loses time or to propose a future state, and writes changes to it. The person sees those changes without reopening anything, and the agent can write a valid map from the companion document alone.

**Why this priority**: collaboration between people and agents is one of ADP's three purposes, and a map an agent can read and propose changes to is an analysis partner, not only a picture.

**Independent Test**: give an agent only the companion document and ask it to write a stream of five steps with times; open the result. Then have it edit an open map.

**Acceptance Scenarios**:

1. **Given** only the companion document, **When** an agent writes a value stream map, **Then** the diagram opens it without a finding.
2. **Given** an open map, **When** an agent changes one step's wait time in the file, **Then** the diagram shows the new value and recomputed totals without being reopened.
3. **Given** an open map, **When** the user changes one step's name in the diagram, **Then** only that name changes in the file.

---

### User Story 6 - A host builds the diagram from its definition (Priority: P1)

A developer of a host finds the Value stream map defined in this repository and builds it from those files alone.

**Why this priority**: every host implements the one specification (constitution, principle I), and no host builds before the definition exists.

**Independent Test**: run the repository's example validator on the definition, its tool description and its examples, with one defect planted at a time.

**Acceptance Scenarios**:

1. **Given** the definition, **When** the example validator and the tool-description checks run, **Then** they pass, and each fails on the defect planted for it.
2. **Given** the worked examples, **When** their totals are computed by hand, **Then** they equal the totals the companion document states for them.

### Edge Cases

- A stream with no steps, only a trigger and an outcome: lead time is zero and a finding says the stream is empty.
- A step not connected to the stream: drawn apart, excluded from the totals, with a finding.
- A cycle that is not marked as rework: a finding, and the totals are not computed until it is resolved.
- Times in mixed units (minutes, hours, days): totals are shown in one unit the user chooses; working-day calendars are out of scope and the companion document says so.
- An activity or practice the vocabulary does not list: kept as written, with a finding, so an agent's text is never lost.
- A file that cannot be read as a whole: the last readable content stays on screen, read-only, with one finding, and the file is not written.

## Requirements *(mandatory)*

### Functional Requirements

- **FR-001** The definition MUST specify element types for a trigger, a step and an outcome, and relation types for a flow, a hand-off, an alternative with a share and rework with a rate.
- **FR-002** A step MUST carry a name, an activity, zero or more practices, a performer, a process time, a wait time and an optional note.
- **FR-003** The document MUST hold its own activity vocabulary and practice list. A new document MUST offer two starting vocabularies, the six service value chain activities and the eight lifecycle activities, and the user MUST be able to change either.
- **FR-004** The definition MUST state how lead time, total process time, flow efficiency and hand-off count are computed, including weighting by alternative shares and rework rates, as expressions a host evaluates rather than code each host writes.
- **FR-005** The diagram MUST mark the steps with the three longest wait times.
- **FR-006** One document MUST be able to hold a current and a future state, and the definition MUST state how their figures are compared.
- **FR-007** The document format MUST be text that a person and an agent can read and write, MUST keep every byte the diagram did not change when it writes, and MUST keep content it cannot follow as written, with a finding.
- **FR-008** The definition MUST be delivered as every diagram type in `definitions/diagrams/` is: its specification, its binding, its tool description with trace, its companion document, a schema where the format needs one, and worked examples, named from the origin `generic/value-stream-map` as `definitions/diagrams/README.md` describes.
- **FR-009** The companion document MUST let a person or an agent write a valid map without reading the specification, and MUST state what the diagram does not do (the list under *What this feature is not*).
- **FR-010** At least two worked examples MUST be delivered: a restore-service stream and a request-fulfilment stream with one alternative and one rework loop, each with a current and a future state and the totals they produce.
- **FR-011** No file of the definition MAY contain text taken from the framework's publications; activity and practice names appear only as editable starting values.
- **FR-012** The tool catalogue (`docs/tools.md` in the standalone repository and the Notion "Tools" database) MUST list the diagram as *Specified* when this feature's definition merges.

### Key Entities

- **Value stream map**: one document; holds the vocabularies and one or two states.
- **State**: current or future; holds one stream.
- **Trigger, Step, Outcome**: the elements of a stream; a step carries the attributes of FR-002.
- **Flow, Hand-off, Alternative, Rework**: the relations between them.
- **Activity, Practice**: the entries of the document's vocabularies.

## Success Criteria *(mandatory)*

### Measurable Outcomes

- **SC-001** The example validator and the tool-description checks pass on the definition and every example, and fail on each planted defect.
- **SC-002** For every worked example, each total the diagram computes equals the hand-calculated value given in the companion document.
- **SC-003** An agent given only the companion document writes a five-step map that opens with no finding, in at least four of five attempts.
- **SC-004** A practitioner can map a six-step stream with times, from an empty document, in under ten minutes.
- **SC-005** After an edit in the diagram, every byte of the file outside the edited value is unchanged.

## Assumptions

- The origin is `generic/value-stream-map`, the same vendor as the timeline diagram, because no single standard owns the notation. The file extension is chosen in the plan.
- The diagram reuses what DISL and FBL already state. If a computed total or the two-state document needs a language construct, the plan records it, and it is delivered in this feature as spec 014 delivered DISL 0.4.
- Times are durations, not timestamps; calendars and working hours are out of scope.
- The first host to build the diagram, and its separate specification, are chosen after this specification is approved.
