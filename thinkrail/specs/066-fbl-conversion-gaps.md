# Feature Specification: FBL 0.10, What the C4, Databricks and Wardley Map Conversions Wait On

**Feature Branch**: `features/066-fbl-conversion-gaps`

**Created**: 2026-10-27

**Status**: Draft

**Input**: The language gaps of three conversion specifications of `etalii.adp.ide.standalone`, which plans with spec-workflow, each read at that repository's commit `6faaeaeb3` under `.spec-workflow/specs/<name>/`: `c4-disl-fbl` (`requirements.md` section C, gaps C1 to C8 and C12; `tasks.md` tasks 13 to 17, which wait on gaps C1 to C7), `databricks-disl-fbl` (`requirements.md` section C, gaps 1 to 7; `tasks.md` tasks 10 to 13, which wait on gaps 1, 2 and 4, task 12 naming stand-ins for gaps 5 to 7) and `wardley-map-disl-fbl` (`requirements.md` section C, gaps 1 to 8; `tasks.md` tasks 11 to 13, which wait on gaps 1 to 4, 7 and 8). The same gaps are recorded, with the fixtures that show them, in this repository's companions `definitions/diagrams/c4-*.md` (*The gaps*, 1 to 8 and 12), `definitions/diagrams/databricks-*.md` (*The gaps*, 1 to 7) and `definitions/diagrams/wardley-map.md` (*Language gaps*, 1 to 8). The maintainer asked that the work those specifications wait on proceed, and ruled on 2026-10-27 that Structurizr DSL and the Wardley map's `.owm` format are bound with FBL's `grammar` family (see Clarifications). The model for this feature is the earlier FBL features [015](../../specs/015-fbl-scalar-as-written), [026](../../specs/026-fbl-insert-empty) and [033](../../specs/033-fbl-insert-constant).

## Context

Three standalone conversions have finished their first stage: their toolboxes, menus, rows and findings are derived from DISL definitions, and their bodies are still read and written by the module's own parser and writer. Their second stage reads and writes the body through an FBL binding, and each of them stopped there because FBL could not state what its format needs. This feature states it.

Each gap was checked against FBL 0.9 (`specifications/fbl/FBL-specification.md`, 2026-10-25), since several FBL versions were published after the conversions were written. Four were answered already in the families the drafts used; the first three are not part of this feature, and the fourth returns under the grammar ruling:

| Gap | Answered by |
|---|---|
| Databricks gap 3, a YAML scalar as written | FBL 0.5: the written text (section 4.1.5), `read`, `quoted` and `fallback` (section 5.2), from feature 015 |
| Databricks gap 6, which rule plans an add when several share a type | Section 6.2: "An add is planned by the first rule of its type, in the binding's order, that has an `insert` whose `when` holds" |
| Databricks gap 6, two containers created by one insert | FBL 0.4: *Missing levels* (section 6.2) |
| Wardley map gap 5, one set-valued attribute stored as the presence of words | FBL 0.7 answers it for a word slot of the `lines` and `blocks` families (sections 4.6, 6.3). Under the grammar ruling, the same reading of a `many` attribute from the repeated nodes of one group is FR-024's |

The conversions' bindings were drafted in the `blocks` and `lines` families. The maintainer ruled that Structurizr DSL (`.dsl`) and the Wardley map's `.owm` format are instead bound with FBL 0.9's `grammar` family (section 4.9), which is extended with what their gaps need, and that the two drafts are redone in it. The family already reads part of what the conversions need: the grammar decides which statements open a block, wherever their brace stands; an ignored token is trivia and a comment; and the contextual lexing of ATD section 3.6 decides where a comment can start. What it lacks is what the requirements below add: it writes no value whose node is absent (it has no `insert-key` or `remove-key`), so a positional argument, a decorator or a block cannot be added to a statement; it writes every value unescaped; it reads a body that does not parse only unit by unit at its top level, so an unclosed block or a broken statement inside one makes the rest of that block unreadable; it does not say which entry owns a comment after a brace or a comment spanning lines, nor that every splice keeps them; its rules cannot define a view (section 4.7's `view` is the `blocks` family's); and no host is required to support it (section 15.1). The Databricks bundle file stays in the `yaml` family, which the ruling does not touch.

### What this feature is not

- **Not a DISL change.** The DISL gaps of the same conversions (C4 gaps C9 to C11 and C13 to C15, Databricks gaps 8 and 9, Wardley map gaps 9 to 12) stay with DISL and are not specified here.
- **Not hierarchical identifiers.** C4 gap C12, an id that depends on `!identifiers hierarchical`, is kept unsupported by the tool on purpose (finding B12: "recorded so that nobody reads the conversion as adding it").
- **Not the host work.** The standalone host implements what FBL gains in the waiting tasks of the three conversions, with this feature's fixtures as their guard; the other hosts follow under their own FBL specifications.
- **Not the standalone documents.** The binding drafts in the appendices of the three conversions' `requirements.md` are documents of `etalii.adp.ide.standalone` and change there, under its own process; this feature gives them the example bindings to follow (FR-033).
- **Not the `blocks` and `lines` families.** The constructs the conversions' drafts gave those families (a trailing comment, a block comment, an escape, positional padding, an unbalanced body) are not added to them by this feature.
- **Not the client's `simulate` player** that Databricks task 14 waits on. It is a runtime gap of the standalone canvas library (DISL 9.6 already defines `simulate`), not a language gap.

## Clarifications

### Session 2026-10-27

The maintainer ruled on the specification's three questions on 2026-10-27; Q1 against its recommended option, Q2 and Q3 for theirs.

- Q: Which FBL family carries the answers for the C4 and Wardley map gaps? → A: The `grammar` family (FBL 0.9, section 4.9), not the recommended `blocks` and `lines`. Structurizr DSL and `.owm` are bound with it, and it is extended with what their gaps need: writing an absent or positional optional value, escapes inside quoted values, recovery nested inside blocks, comments after a brace or a statement and across lines read as trivia and kept, a brace on the next line and braces that do not balance, inserting a key or an argument, and the view and legacy-layout questions where they fall to the family. Every host that binds these formats supports the family (FR-037); the version history, the conformance classes and the fixtures say so (FR-030, FR-032); and the conversions' binding drafts are redone in the family (FR-033).
- Q: Does FBL 0.10 also take the gaps that only retire a named stand-in (FR-012, FR-013, FR-019 to FR-022, FR-025), or only those a waiting task names? → A: Every gap, the stand-in ones included.
- Q: Is a registration whose `view` names no view of the body reported as a finding, or opened in silence as the tool does today? → A: Empty and silent: it shows no view's content and no finding is reported (FR-011), as C4's question Q4 ("switched off, and today's order kept") was ruled.

### Session 2026-10-30

The maintainer ruled on one question raised after the feature's three pull requests had merged, by a decision card, and so amended the feature in place (FR-038, User Story 1 scenarios 12 and 13). A second question, raised the same day by the standalone Databricks conversion, was ruled on its card for its recommended answer (FR-039, User Story 2 scenarios 10 and 11).

- Q: How should C4 handle several tags written in one quoted argument? The standalone C4 module splits the tags `"Extra, More"` into two tags, while FBL 0.12 reads them as one; the C4 companions record this as gap 16. → A: A new FBL construct, amending spec 066 with a schema change and a fixture, not leaving the gap open nor accepting FBL's one-tag reading (Peter, 2026-10-30). It is FBL 0.13 (FR-038).
- Q: Should FBL be extended so that an insert of the `yaml` family can add an entry under a new key of a mapping, such as a job under `resources.jobs`? databricks-disl-fbl task 12 found that a resource cannot be added through the binding: the standalone library refuses with "The /resources/jobs of this file is not a list a Resource can be added to.", since FBL 0.13 states an insert into a sequence and none into a mapping. → A: Extend FBL (Peter, 2026-10-30): an insert adds a member under a new key, as FBL 0.14 (FR-039), the same construct covering the members of a JSON object; the module does not keep adding resources itself.

## User Scenarios & Testing *(mandatory)*

### User Story 1 - A Structurizr DSL workspace is read and written through a declared binding (Priority: P1)

A tool engineer writes one binding in the `grammar` family for a Structurizr DSL workspace (`.dsl`), whose grammar names the DSL's statements, and the six C4 readings share it. Every workspace the C4 tool opens today opens through that binding with the same elements, views and attributes: comments after a brace, after a statement and across lines change nothing that is read and survive every edit; a quoted name holding an escaped quote reads as the quote; a body being typed, whose braces do not yet balance or in which one statement inside a block does not parse, still shows everything else that can be read; and the view a registration selects brings its own statements (`include`, `exclude`, `title`, `autoLayout`, a dynamic view's interactions) into the reading. A user who sets a technology on `container "Web"` gets `container "Web" "" "Java"`, with every comment and every other argument of the line kept. Positions kept in an existing `<base>.layout.json` apply and are written back as FBL states.

**Why this priority**: it unblocks five waiting tasks (c4-disl-fbl 13 to 17), the most of the three, for six tool types at once, and most of the grammar family's new constructs (comments, nested recovery, absent values, inserted parts) also serve the Wardley map.

**Independent Test**: a round-trip fixture per construct, under the Structurizr binding redone in the `grammar` family of FBL 0.10, reads each of the companion's fixtures (`comments-everywhere.dsl`, `findings/trailing-comments.dsl`, `findings/block-comments.dsl`, `findings/escaped-quote.dsl`, `findings/unbalanced.dsl`, `findings/view-statements.dsl`, `findings/sidecar-keys.dsl` with its `.layout.json`, `findings/view-without-key.dsl`) and states its elements and the splices of each edit; the validator replays them, and fails them with one planted wrong offset. The existing fixture `structurizr-open-block` passes under the redone binding with its input and its expected bytes unchanged.

**Acceptance Scenarios**:

1. **Given** `model { // the model`, **When** the body is read, **Then** the statement opens the `model` block, the braces balance, the comment is part of no value and is owned by the `model` statement rather than by the first statement inside the block.
2. **Given** a statement followed by `// …` or `# …` outside a quoted string, **When** one of its values is set, **Then** only that value's bytes change and the comment is kept as it was.
3. **Given** a `/* … */` comment spanning three lines that holds a declaration and a brace, **When** the body is read, **Then** nothing inside it is a statement, an element or a brace; **and When** the element on the line next to it is removed, **Then** the comment stays or goes with it as FBL states for the entry that owns it.
4. **Given** `person "Jo \"the user\""`, **When** it is read, **Then** the name is `Jo "the user"`; **and When** the name is set to `Parity "name"`, **Then** it is written `"Parity \"name\""` and the set is not refused.
5. **Given** `container "Web"` with no description, **When** its technology (the third argument) is set to `Java`, **Then** the line becomes `container "Web" "" "Java"`, written before any brace and before any trailing comment, and an undo restores `container "Web"` byte for byte.
6. **Given** a statement that opens a block, **When** an argument of it is set or written where it was absent, **Then** the block's opening and every byte of the block are kept.
7. **Given** a binding that recovers inside blocks, and a workspace missing its last two `}` in whose `model` block one statement does not parse, **When** it is read, **Then** every element up to the end of the body is read, the siblings after the statement that does not parse included, that statement's text is kept and reported at its line, the body stays editable, the missing braces are reported, and an add into a block that is never closed is refused with a reason.
8. **Given** a registration whose `view` names the container view `containers`, **When** it is read, **Then** the reading's diagram attributes are that view's `include`, `exclude`, `title`, `autoLayout` and scope, and no other view's statements reach it, while every view is still an element of the model.
9. **Given** `<base>.layout.json` whose element keys differ in case from the ids, **When** the reading opens, **Then** every stored position applies, matched as FBL states, and a move writes the file back in the number form FBL states.
10. **Given** a registration whose `view` names no view of the body, **When** it opens, **Then** it shows no view's content, no finding is reported, and a move is refused with a reason and writes nothing.
11. **Given** a host that does not support the `grammar` family, **When** it opens a workspace through the binding, **Then** it opens it read-only, if it can read it at all, and says why (section 15.1).
12. *(Amendment of 2026-10-30, FR-038)* **Given** `tags "Extra, More"` in a software system's block, **When** the body is read, **Then** the system's tags are `Extra` and `More`, after the tags of its declaration, as Structurizr and the C4 tool read them; `"Existing System"` is one tag, since only a comma cuts.
13. *(Amendment of 2026-10-30, FR-038)* **Given** a binding whose tags are writable and an argument `"One ,Two,  Three"`, **When** a set removes `Two`, adds a tag or changes one, **Then** only the bytes of that tag and of one separator change: the removal writes `"One,  Three"`, an added tag is written after the last with the separator, and every byte of a tag the set keeps, and between two tags it keeps, stays; an undo restores the argument byte for byte.

### User Story 2 - One Databricks bundle file is three editable readings (Priority: P2)

A tool engineer writes one binding for a Databricks bundle file that the bundle, job and pipeline readings share. A user opens `databricks.yml` as the bundle through `databricks.adp` and as one pipeline through `databricks.pipeline.adp`; both readings are editable, an edit in one shows in the other without a reload, and one undo history serves both. The bundle reading sees every resource, a job registration shows the job its `resource:` header names, and a pipeline registration the pipeline its header names. A user can add a task to `tasks: []` and a dependency to `depends_on: [{task_key: a}]`, and the file stays valid YAML.

**Why this priority**: it unblocks four waiting tasks (databricks-disl-fbl 10 to 13) for three tool types.

**Independent Test**: round-trip fixtures over the example `examples/lakehouse/databricks.yml` and the fixtures `two-jobs-two-pipelines.yml`, `flow-collections.yml` and `flow-dependencies.yml` read each reading's elements and plan the edits the transcript makes, and the validator replays them; a fixture shows that a registration of each of the three readings over one body is editable.

**Acceptance Scenarios**:

1. **Given** `resources.pipelines.bronze_to_gold`, **When** the bundle reading and the pipeline reading read it through one binding, **Then** it is a resource to the first and the pipeline itself to the second, and neither reading holds an element of a type its metamodel does not declare.
2. **Given** both readings open, **When** a user renames a task in the job reading, **Then** the bundle reading shows the new name without a reload, and one undo in either restores it in both.
3. **Given** two job registrations and two pipeline registrations of one file, **When** each opens, **Then** each shows and edits the resource its own `resource:` header names, or the first of its kind in document order without one, and the bundle registration selects none.
4. **Given** `tasks: []`, **When** a task is added, **Then** the file holds the task as FBL states, reads back with one task, and the undo restores `tasks: []` byte for byte.
5. **Given** `depends_on: [{task_key: a}]`, **When** a dependency on `b` is added, **Then** it is written inside the one-line sequence after `, `, and removing it restores the line.
6. **Given** a task added to a file without a `tasks` section that the binding refuses, **When** the add is refused, **Then** the user sees the sentence the binding gives ("The job has no tasks section to add into."), not one built from type names.
7. **Given** a pipeline that has `target` and no `schema`, **When** it is read, **Then** its schema is the value of `target`; **and When** the schema is set, **Then** it is written to `schema` and `target` is removed.
8. **Given** a new document named `My_Job.yml`, **When** it is created from the template, **Then** its key is `my_job`.
9. **Given** a library added to a YAML pipeline, **When** it is written, **Then** it is a nested mapping (`- notebook:` with `path:` below it) that reads back as the library added.
10. *(Amendment of 2026-10-30, FR-039)* **Given** `resources:` holding `jobs:` with the job `nightly_ingest` and then `pipelines:`, **When** the bundle reading adds a job keyed `weekly_rollup`, **Then** `weekly_rollup:` is written as a member of `jobs` right after `nightly_ingest`'s lines and before `pipelines:`, indented as `nightly_ingest:` is, with the job's skeleton below it, every other byte of the file is kept, the bundle reads back with the new job, and one undo restores the file byte for byte; **and Given** a file with no `resources:` or with `resources:` and no `jobs:`, **When** a job is added, **Then** the missing levels are written first, at the end of the body and of `resources` respectively, in the same edit.
11. *(Amendment of 2026-10-30, FR-039)* **Given** a job keyed `nightly_ingest` already in the file, **When** a job of that key or of no key is added, **Then** the add is refused with the sentence the binding gives, and nothing is written; **and When** the job `nightly_ingest` is removed, **Then** its key's line and every line of its value go, and `jobs:` with them when nothing else is in it.

### User Story 3 - A Wardley map is read and written through a declared binding (Priority: P3)

A tool engineer writes a binding in the `grammar` family for the Wardley map's `.owm` format. Every map the tool opens today opens through it with the same elements: a pipeline whose `{` stands on the line below its statement owns that block, a statement such as a title that happens to end in `{` opens nothing, a pipeline that is never closed keeps its children and the map stays editable, a `//` comment after a statement is kept by every edit and a `//` after a colon is not a comment, and `label [-30, 12]`, `( market )`, `inertia` and `url(name)` are read wherever they stand after the coordinates and can be set, added and cleared one by one. Every id the tool stored in `<base>.identities.json`, an array of `{id, kind, key}`, stays the id of the same element, and two components of one name each keep an id of their own.

**Why this priority**: it unblocks three waiting tasks (wardley-map-disl-fbl 11 to 13) for one tool type; most of the grammar family's new constructs it needs are delivered with User Story 1.

**Independent Test**: round-trip fixtures under an `.owm` binding written in the `grammar` family of FBL 0.10 read the companion's fixtures (`pipelines-both-forms.owm`, `unclosed-pipeline.owm`, `brace-ending-statements.owm`, `comments-everywhere.owm`, `trailing-comments.owm`, `lenient-trailer.owm`, `annotations-and-labels.owm`, `same-key-twice.owm`, `duplicate-names.owm`) and the identities files of `examples/example 2/`, and state their elements, ids and the splices of each edit; the validator replays them.

**Acceptance Scenarios**:

1. **Given** `pipeline Kettle` followed by a line holding only `{`, **When** the body is read, **Then** the pipeline owns that block and its `component` lines are its children; **and When** a pipeline is added, **Then** its brace is written in the form the binding declares, on its own line, and the undo removes the whole pipeline.
2. **Given** `title A map {`, **When** the body is read under a grammar in which only a pipeline opens a block, **Then** the title opens nothing, its `{` is part of its text, and the braces balance.
3. **Given** a pipeline that is never closed, **When** the body is read under a binding that recovers inside blocks, **Then** its children are read, the map is editable, the missing brace is reported, and an add into that pipeline is refused with a reason.
4. **Given** `component Tea [0.2, 0.8] // hot` and `url(site) [0.1, 0.2] https://example.com`, **When** the coordinates of Tea are changed, **Then** only the numbers change and the comment is kept; and the `//` after `https:` is not a comment.
5. **Given** a component written `component Tea [0.2, 0.8] inertia label [-30, 12] ( market )`, **When** it is read, **Then** the label offset, the decorator and the inertia are read wherever they stand in the trailer, and setting one changes only its own bytes; **and Given** `component Milk [0.3, 0.6]`, **When** its label offset is set, **Then** `label [x, y]` is written at the place the binding declares, and clearing it removes that segment and one separator and nothing else.
6. **Given** `annotation 1 [[0.43,0.49],[0.08,0.79]] text`, **When** it is read, **Then** it yields two positions, each with its two numbers, each writable on its own.
7. **Given** `annotations-and-labels.identities.json`, **When** the map opens through the binding, **Then** every element has the id the file stores for its kind and key, and no element gets a new id.
8. **Given** two components named `Tea`, **When** the map is read, **Then** each has an id of its own, stored, and the same when the map is opened again, the later holder's entry carrying its ordinal in its key.
9. **Given** a component whose name is part of its identity key, **When** it is renamed, **Then** its identity entry moves to the new key in the same edit, and an undo moves it back.

### Edge Cases

- **A comment marker inside a quoted string** (`"a // b"`, `"#1"`) is part of the string and never starts a comment.
- **A comment that opens on a statement's line and closes lines later** (`model { /* start` … `end */`): the statement and its brace are read, and nothing inside the comment is.
- **A block comment that is never closed** runs to the end of the body, is reported, and the body is not unreadable for it.
- **A comment inside the span of a node a slot reads** (`"a" /* x */ "b"` read as one value): FBL states whether the value's text includes the comment and whether a set of that value keeps it or is refused; it is never silently dropped.
- **An escape at the end of a quoted value** (`"a\"`): the value does not end at the escaped quote; a value that then never ends is text that does not parse, kept and reported as the statement it stands in, not the body.
- **A value the escape cannot write** (a line break in a one-line quoted value): the set is refused by the family's re-read check (section 4.9), and nothing is written.
- **A positional argument set on a statement whose earlier arguments are present but empty** (`container "Web" ""`): the empty argument is replaced, not padded again.
- **Clearing the last positional argument** leaves the padded empty arguments before it as they are; FBL states whether trailing empty arguments are removed.
- **A stray `}` with no open block** under a binding that recovers inside blocks is unbound, kept and reported, and closes nothing.
- **A statement that does not parse at the top level**: FBL 0.9's top-level recovery stands; the nested recovery of FR-002 changes nothing for it.
- **Two entries of a legacy layout whose element keys differ only in case**: FBL states which one applies.
- **A registration with a `view` header and a binding that defines no views**: as FBL 0.9 says, the header is kept and the reading shows the body.
- **A Databricks reading over a body whose resource its header names does not exist**: the reading opens empty and says why, as a missing `resource` does today; it is not unreadable.
- **A YAML flow sequence written over several lines**: it is not a one-line collection; FBL states how an insert into it is planned or refused.
- **An insert into a flow mapping** (`{}`): not needed by these formats; it stays refused.
- **A fallback slot present together with the slot it falls back from**: the first slot is read, and the fallback is removed when the attribute is written.
- **An `.owm` statement whose trailer holds one segment twice** (two labels): the first is read, as the tool does, and a set changes the first.
- **An identities entry of the legacy file whose kind no rule declares**: it is kept as written and reported as stale.
- **A rename to a name another holder already has**: refused, as today (section 5.7).
- **A host that supports the `grammar` family at 0.9 only** and opens a 0.10 binding that uses a construct of this feature: it treats the binding as one whose family it does not support (FR-037).
- *(FR-038)* **An argument with an empty piece or nothing but white space** (`"A,,B"`, `""`, `" "`): an empty piece is no item, so `"A,,B"` holds two and `""` none; a tag added to an argument that holds none is written as its value.
- *(FR-038)* **A set that removes every tag of an argument**: the argument goes as a removed node of a `many` slot does, with the white space before it; FBL states it.
- *(FR-038)* **A separator that the slot's escape could write** (a backslash in it): the binding is refused when it is loaded, so that the items do not depend on whether the escapes are resolved before the cut.
- *(FR-039)* **A key that would need quoting** (`my job`, `true`): it is written as section 6.3 writes a new string value, quoted where a plain scalar would read otherwise, and compared with the mapping's keys as written text.
- *(FR-039)* **A mapping written in flow form** (`jobs: {}`): it takes no insert, as the edge case of a flow mapping above says; a key written alone (`jobs:`) takes the member as the first of a block mapping below it.
- *(FR-039)* **A rule that adds a member and binds nothing to its key's capture**: the binding is refused when it is loaded, since the add would have no key to write.

## Requirements *(mandatory)*

Every construct below is stated by what a binding can declare and what a host then reads and writes; the constructs' names and shapes are the plan's. Where the standalone drafts gave a construct a placeholder name (`trailingComment`, `blockComment`, `escape`, `pad`, `unbalanced`, `selectedViewOnly` in c4-disl-fbl's appendix), those were names in the `blocks` family, which these formats are no longer bound with; the plan names each construct as the `grammar` family needs it. Each requirement names the gap it answers and the story it serves. By the ruling of 2026-10-27 the requirements of User Stories 1 and 3 are stated for the `grammar` family (section 4.9), those of User Story 2 for the `yaml` family, and every gap is in scope: the requirements marked *retires a stand-in* answer gaps that no waiting task needs, because the conversion keeps named module code for them meanwhile.

### Key Entities

- **Comment**: an ignored token of the grammar that holds a character other than whitespace; trivia, owned by the entry FBL states, after a statement, after a brace, or across lines.
- **Nested recovery**: reading a body that does not parse in part inside its blocks, so that one broken statement or one missing brace leaves every other entry readable and editable.
- **Absent part**: an optional part of a rule's grammar that an entry does not hold, which a set writes and a clear removes.
- **Positional argument**: an optional value of a statement whose meaning is its position, so that an absent earlier argument is written empty to keep a later one in place.
- **Escape**: the character sequence that writes a quote, and the escape character itself, inside a quoted value.
- **Selected view**: the view a registration's `view` header selects, whose statements the reading reads into its diagram.
- **Reading's origin set**: the origins a rule or a resource capture applies to, within a binding several tool types share.
- **Identity kind**: the key space of an `identities:` entry, declared by a rule, in which a holder's ordinal tells repeated natural keys apart.
- **Legacy identities file**: the Wardley map's `<base>.identities.json`, an array of `{id, kind, key}`.
- **Split slot** *(amendment of 2026-10-30)*: a slot whose one node holds several items of a `many` attribute, cut at a separator the binding declares, each item with a span of its own.
- **Member insert** *(amendment of 2026-10-30)*: an insert that adds an entry to a mapping or object as a new member, under the key the add gives the attribute its rule reads from the key.
