# Feature Specification: A Built-in Person Shape in DISL

**Feature Branch**: `features/031-disl-person-shape`

**Created**: 2026-10-19

**Status**: Implemented as DISL 0.5 (2026-10-20, [plan.md](../../specs/031-disl-person-shape/plan.md), [tasks.md](../../specs/031-disl-person-shape/tasks.md)).

**Input**: The maintainer's ruling of 2026-10-19, on the decision card "Add to DISL": add `person` to DISL as a built-in node shape, beside `cylinder`, in the DISL specification's shape catalogue (section 6.7 of `specifications/disl/DISL-specification.md` and Appendix B.2), and in the JSON Schema `disl.schema.json` where shapes are enumerated. It closes gap C1 of task 11 of the standalone specification `c4-disl-fbl`: the C4 diagram tool type needs a person drawn as C4 draws one, a rounded box with a round head above its top edge and its label inside the box.

## Context

**What exists.** DISL 0.4 offers about forty built-in shapes (section 6.7), with their parameter defaults in Appendix B.2. Among them is `actor`, a stick figure whose label lies below the figure. It is a different shape: C4's person is a box that carries its name, description and type inside, with a head drawn above it.

Because DISL has no such shape, the six C4 definitions (`definitions/diagrams/c4-*.dis`) each declare a custom composite shape, `c4Person`: a circle of radius 10 whose centre sits 8 above the top edge, under a rounded box of radius 8 over the bounds, with only the box contributing to the outline. The DISL example `specifications/disl/c4-container.dis` declares its own `c4Person` too, with the head's centre 18 above the top edge instead of 8. A host can only draw these as a generic composite, and the standalone host, which draws C4 from its own shape library, cannot map the custom shape to the person shape it already has (standalone pull request #504 added `person` to its library and deliberately left the DISL catalogue entry out, because DISL had none).

**What this feature adds.** One built-in shape, `person`, stated in DISL's own terms: its geometry relative to a node's bounds, its outline for connections and hit testing, its text area, and its one parameter with its default. A tool engineer then writes `"shape": "person"` and every host that implements DISL's built-in shapes draws the same figure.

**Why in the language.** Principle I: a need a host discovers comes back to the specification before hosts diverge on it. Two definitions of the same figure already differ in this repository (head centre 8 above in `definitions/`, 18 above in the example), and a host that recognises a person only by matching a custom shape's parts would be implementing a format of its own.

**Words.** Those of [docs/terminology.md](../../docs/terminology.md). **Bounds** are a node's box, as DISL uses the word in sections 6.7 to 6.9 (x, y, width w, height h, y growing downwards). The **body** is the person's box; the **head** is its circle. **Above** means towards smaller y.

## Clarifications

### Session 2026-10-19

- Q: Which parameters does `person` have? (FR-003) → A: Corner only: the body's corner radius, `radius`, default 8, as `roundedRect` has; the head is fixed at radius 10 with its centre 8 above the top edge.
- Q: Does the head join the outline that relations anchor on? (FR-004) → A: Body, head clicks: relations attach to the body only, and a click on the head still selects the node.

## User Scenarios & Testing *(mandatory)*

### User Story 1 - A tool engineer draws a person with one word (Priority: P1)

A tool engineer specifying a C4 diagram, or any diagram with people in it, writes `"shape": "person"` in a node notation and gets the C4 person: a rounded box with the label inside and a round head above it. No custom shape is declared and no geometry is written.

**Why this priority**: it is the ruling. Without it every C4 definition carries its own copy of the figure, and the copies already disagree.

**Independent Test**: a reader of section 6.7 and Appendix B.2 alone can draw `person` for bounds of 160 by 100 and give the head's centre, its radius, the body's corner radius and where the label goes, without looking at any host or definition.

**Acceptance Scenarios**:

1. **Given** a node notation with `"shape": "person"` and bounds at (x, y) of width w and height h, **When** a runtime draws the node with the defaults, **Then** the body is a rounded rectangle exactly over the bounds with corner radius 8, and the head is a circle of radius 10 whose centre is at (x + w/2, y − 8).
2. **Given** the same node, **When** it is drawn, **Then** the body is painted over the head, so the part of the head that lies inside the bounds is hidden and the head shows as a cap above the top edge.
3. **Given** the same node, **When** its main label is laid out, **Then** it lies in the body, as for `roundedRect`, and not beside or below the figure.
4. **Given** a node notation with `"shape": "actor"`, **When** it is drawn, **Then** it is the stick figure it was before; `actor` does not change.

---

### User Story 2 - Connections and pointing behave as C4 readers expect (Priority: P1)

A user connects a person to a software system, and later clicks the person to select it. The connection ends on the person's box, as on every other C4 card, and a click on the head selects the person as a click on the box does.

**Why this priority**: the outline is part of what a built-in shape defines (section 6.7: every built-in shape defines its outline for edge anchoring and hit testing). Two hosts that anchor differently draw different diagrams from the same definition.

**Independent Test**: for a person node and a relation arriving from directly above and from the side, the specification alone says where each relation ends and which points select the node.

**Acceptance Scenarios**:

1. **Given** a relation that ends at a person, **When** the runtime anchors it, **Then** it ends on the body's outline, the rounded rectangle, by the same rules as for `roundedRect` (section 6.10).
2. **Given** a point inside the visible part of the head, **When** the user points or clicks there, **Then** the hit falls on the person node.
3. **Given** a point outside both the body and the head but inside a rectangle that would enclose them, **When** the user clicks there, **Then** the person is not hit.

---

### User Story 3 - The C4 definitions and the hosts can use it (Priority: P2)

The maintainer of the C4 definitions replaces each `c4Person` custom shape by `"shape": "person"`, and a host's DISL shape catalogue gains one entry mapping the built-in to the figure it draws, without either one waiting on a further language change.

**Why this priority**: it is what the shape is for, but the edits land in follow-ups (see Scope), so this story only requires that nothing in the language stands in their way.

**Independent Test**: a C4 definition in which `"shape": "c4Person"` is replaced by `"shape": "person"` and the custom shape removed validates against `disl.schema.json` and, with the defaults, describes the same figure as the custom shape it replaces.

**Acceptance Scenarios**:

1. **Given** the `c4Person` shape of `definitions/diagrams/c4-*.dis`, **When** it is compared with `person` at its defaults, **Then** head radius, head position, body corner radius, painting order and outline are the same.
2. **Given** a specification that declares a custom shape named `person`, **When** it is opened after this feature, **Then** its own shape is drawn, as section 6.8 already says of a custom shape that shares a built-in's name.

### Edge Cases

- **The head lies outside the bounds.** With the defaults the head reaches 18 above the top edge. The bounds stay the body: a node's size, position, snapping, containment and layout refer to the bounds as for every shape, as the `callout` pointer already may lie outside them. Anything that measures what is drawn rather than the bounds, such as fitting the diagram to the view or exporting it, includes the head, so the head is never cut off.
- **A node narrower than the head.** When w is less than twice the head's radius the head is wider than the body. The head keeps its radius and stays centred; the shape is still drawn, and no finding is raised.
- **A node smaller than twice the corner radius.** The body's corner radius is limited to half the smaller side of the bounds, so the body never turns inside out; the specification states this for `person`, since section 6.7 does not state it for `roundedRect`.
- **Strokes and fills.** Head and body take the node's one style: the same fill, stroke and states, so a selected or highlighted person shows it on both. The body's stroke is drawn over the head where the two cross, since the body is painted after the head.
- **A corner radius bound to data.** The shape's one parameter, `radius`, is Bindable like every built-in parameter (section 6.7); a person whose `radius` follows an attribute or a CEL expression is drawn with the evaluated value. A negative radius is not a valid value. The head does not follow it.
- **A runtime at the Core conformance level.** Section 15's Core level names a few built-in shapes; `person` joins the Standard level's "all built-in shapes", as `cylinder` and `actor` are, and Core is unchanged.

## Requirements *(mandatory)*

### Functional Requirements

- **FR-001**: Section 6.7 of the DISL specification **MUST** list `person` as a built-in shape, in the table beside `cylinder`, with its parameter and a note that distinguishes it from `actor` (a box with the label inside and a head above, against a stick figure with the label below).
- **FR-002**: The specification **MUST** state the geometry relative to the node's bounds: the body is a rounded rectangle exactly over the bounds; the head is a circle centred horizontally on the bounds, whose centre lies a stated distance above the top edge; the body is painted after the head; the body's corner radius is limited to half the smaller side of the bounds.
- **FR-003**: The shape **MUST** have exactly one parameter, `radius`, the body's corner radius, as `roundedRect` has, and Appendix B.2 **MUST** list its default, 8. The head **MUST** be fixed and not a parameter: radius 10, centre 8 above the top edge, in canvas units.
- **FR-004**: The specification **MUST** state the shape's outline for edge anchoring and its area for hit testing, as section 6.7 requires of every built-in shape: relations anchor on the body's rounded rectangle alone, and hit testing covers the body and the head together, so a click on the head selects the node. Section 6.7's sentence that a built-in's outline serves both edge anchoring and hit testing **MUST** say that `person` is the exception.
- **FR-005**: The specification **MUST** state the shape's text area: the body, as for `roundedRect`, minus the node's padding. No part of the head is in it.
- **FR-006**: The specification **MUST** say that the bounds are the body, that the head is drawn outside them, and that a measure of what a diagram draws includes the head.
- **FR-007**: `actor` and every other existing built-in shape **MUST** keep their meaning, and a custom shape named `person` **MUST** keep shadowing the built-in (section 6.8).
- **FR-008**: `disl.schema.json` **MUST** accept `"shape": "person"` and `{ "type": "person", "params": { … } }` wherever a shape reference is allowed. Where the schema names built-in shapes, in an enumeration or in a description, it **MUST** name `person` too; where it accepts any name, as `$defs/ShapeRef` does today, it needs no other change.
- **FR-009**: At least one worked example under `specifications/disl/` **MUST** use `person` and validate against the schema (Principle II). `c4-container.dis`, which today declares its own person, is the natural one.
- **FR-010**: The DISL document **MUST** record the addition in the list of changes of the version that carries it, and say that every specification valid before stays valid with the same meaning; the only document whose drawing changes is one that names `person` without declaring it, which was an unknown shape before.
- **FR-011**: No other specification language changes. DID stores no shapes, and FBL binds no notation.

### Key Entities

- **Body**: the rounded rectangle over the node's bounds; carries the label, the anchoring outline and the node's style.
- **Head**: the circle above the body's top edge, centred on it horizontally; drawn first, part of the hit area, outside the bounds and the text area.
- **Person shape parameter**: `radius`, the body's corner radius, default 8 in Appendix B.2. The head's radius (10) and its distance above the top edge (8) are fixed lengths of the shape, not parameters.

## Scope and Dependencies

**In scope**: section 6.7, section 15's conformance wording if it lists shapes, Appendix B.2, the change list of the DISL document, `disl.schema.json` where it names shapes, and one example under `specifications/disl/`.

**Consumers, as follow-ups outside this feature**:

- `definitions/diagrams/c4-*.dis` in this repository: each `c4Person` custom shape is replaced by `"shape": "person"`. This is a change of its own, after this one is merged.
- `etalii.adp.ide.standalone`: its DISL shape catalogue (`src/client/src/canvas/library/disl/shapeCatalog.ts`) gets the `person` entry that pull request #504 left out, mapping to the `person` shape its library already draws, under task 11 (gap C1) of its specification `c4-disl-fbl`.
- Other hosts (`etalii.adp.ide.vscode`, `etalii.adp.ide.intellij`, `etalii.adp.ide.notion`) draw `person` when their own specifications take up the C4 tool type or the Standard set of shapes; this feature asks nothing of them.

## Success Criteria *(mandatory)*

### Measurable Outcomes

- **SC-001**: A reader of section 6.7 and Appendix B.2 alone, given bounds of 160 by 100 at the origin, states the head's centre (80, −8), its radius 10 and the body's corner radius 8, and draws the figure the C4 definitions' `c4Person` draws today.
- **SC-002**: The same reader says, for a relation arriving from above and for a click on the head, where the relation ends and whether the person is hit, with one answer each.
- **SC-003**: `python .github/scripts/validate-examples.py` passes with the new example, and every existing example and definition passes unchanged.
- **SC-004**: The standalone host's catalogue entry and the C4 definitions' switch to `"shape": "person"` can each be written from the merged specification without a further question to the maintainer.

## Assumptions

- The maintainer's ruling of 2026-10-19 decides that `person` is a DISL built-in; this feature writes down what it is.
- The defaults are the figure the C4 definitions and the standalone library draw today (head radius 10, centre 8 above, corner radius 8), not the example's 18 above; the example adopts the defaults and its head moves down by 10.
- Lengths are in canvas units and do not scale with the node's size, as the C4 definitions draw them; a person of any size has the same head.
- The body's outline for anchoring excludes the head, as the custom shapes and the standalone library have it (ruled on 2026-10-19, see Clarifications).
- Which DISL version carries the addition (an amendment of the 0.4 draft or a new 0.5) is the plan's to propose; Principle IV allows either before 1.0, and FR-010 holds for both.
