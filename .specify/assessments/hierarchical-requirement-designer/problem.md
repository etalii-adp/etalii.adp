## Problem Statement

A person who works with agents on requirements has those requirements only as text: Markdown that the agent reads and writes, kept flat and per feature, in which the person can find what concerns them only by skimming and by searching for words, and which the person has to read in full to judge. That is slow, and it does not show how the requirements fit together. What the requirements are about (the subject, below) has a structure of its own: the case on record is the software application, which is hierarchical in nature (UI, integration, backend, data, processes). For content of that kind people interpret and interact better with a non-text presentation than with text, so the one form that suits the agent is the form that suits the person least, and there is no one place that follows the subject's structure in which the two can discuss its requirements.

The problem is not limited to software applications. Asked on 2026-10-13 whether requirements that concern something else are out of scope, the maintainer answered that nothing is out of scope by default and that this is about any kind of requirement engineering (see Resolved, under Open Questions). The software application is the case the motive names and the only one the intake and the research have evidence on; that requirements of another subject are hierarchical in the same way is taken from the answer, not shown.

Why now is a trend and not an occasion. The maintainer gave an observation as the motive, not an event, a project or a deadline (intake, Origin & Context), and asked on 2026-10-13 what makes this pressing now answered "The fact that agent based development is increasingly being used" (see Resolved, under Open Questions). So the problem presses because the way of working that causes it is spreading: the more work is done with agents, the more requirements reach a person only as the text an agent reads and writes, and the more people that holds for. The research agrees on the direction, that the practice is moving towards more agent-written requirement text, and towards documents that are kept after the work is done, which the popular tools do not yet aim at (research, Market & Context; one practitioner's article, confidence medium). A trend gives no date: nothing on record says by when this has to be solved, or what is lost if it is solved later. The maintainer has placed it accordingly: asked on 2026-10-13 how it sequences with the open work on the standalone host, the answer was "After that work" (see Resolved, under Open Questions). So the problem presses, and it does not press ahead of what is already under way.

## Affected Users & Stakeholders

- **Users**: the maintainer, as a person who reads and judges agent-written requirements every day — the organization keeps 25 Spec Kit feature folders in this repository and 34 spec-workflow specs in the standalone host, all as text (research, Users & Demand; confidence high). That this hurts enough to act on is a stated want, not an observed cost (research; confidence low). Those 59 specifications show the maintainer's experience of the problem and are not themselves in scope: the maintainer answered on 2026-10-13 that the concept has no relation to them and is intended for use outside this repository (see Resolved, under Open Questions), so the requirements the maintainer would keep this way are others, and none is on record.
- **Users**: agents that read and write the requirements — they are the other party to the dialogue, and whatever is done for the person must leave the text something an agent can read and write as text (intake, Idea and Origin & Context).
- **Users**: people involved in requirement engineering, and among them especially those who are required to work together with LLM agents — named by the maintainer on 2026-10-13 as the people who experience the problem besides the maintainer (see Resolved, under Open Questions). They are a group the maintainer names, not people who were asked or observed: no one of them is on record by name, and the practitioners outside the organization who report the burden of reviewing agent-written Markdown (Böckeler: "very verbose and tedious to review") show that the problem exists among such people, not that they are users of ADP (research, Users & Demand; confidence low for the demand).
- **Stakeholders**: the maintainer, as the one who raises, decides and builds — the same person as the only user known by name, so no one else's interest checks the idea (research, Evidence Against).
- **Stakeholders**: the tool engineers and hosts of ADP — the research finds that the idea as captured would touch DED, DESL, FBL and the registration, which every host must implement and the constitution allows only for "a current tool's need" (research, Data & Constraints). They carry the cost of a solution; they do not experience the problem. Asked on 2026-10-13 whether the need justifies that cost, the maintainer, who is the tool engineer concerned, answered that it does (see Resolved, under Open Questions).

## Goals

- A person can take in and judge the requirements of a subject, an application or anything else requirements are drawn up for, by that subject's structure, without reading the whole text.
- A person and an agent can discuss the same requirements, each in the form that suits them, and what one changes the other sees.
- One place holds the requirements of a subject across its parts, and is kept as the subject changes, where today they are spread over per-feature files.
- A person or an agent can point at one requirement, or one part of the subject, and the other knows which is meant, also after the requirements have been reorganised.
- Questions, remarks and warnings about a requirement stay with that requirement, where the person who has to act on them sees them.
- A person can narrow what they look at to the part of the subject, or the kind of requirement, that concerns them at that moment.

## Non-Goals

- Hosts other than the standalone host: the maintainer answered "First only the standalone" (intake, Clarifications). Which hosts follow, and when, is not part of this problem.
- Relations between things: a requirement may point at another requirement, but showing how requirements connect is what a diagram is for, and the idea is of a designer (intake; `docs/terminology.md` as cited in research, Data & Constraints).
- Taking the text away from the agent: the agent keeps reading and writing text. A presentation for people that agents cannot work beside is outside the problem as stated.
- Solving the Knowledge designer's problem again: only its looks are named as a model, "its looks only" (intake, Clarifications).
- Reducing the amount of agent-written requirement text: asked on 2026-10-13 whether that is out of scope, the maintainer answered "Volume is better consumable when presented correctly." So the amount of text is taken as given, and the problem is the form in which it reaches the person; getting agents to write less is not part of it (see Resolved, under Open Questions).
- The requirement files the organization already uses (Spec Kit's `spec.md`, spec-workflow's `requirements.md`): asked on 2026-10-13 how this relates to them, the maintainer answered "no relation. this is a whole new concept intended for use outside this repository." So those files are not what is to change: they are neither to be read nor to be replaced, and the way this repository and the standalone host specify their own work stays as it is (see Resolved, under Open Questions). They remain the evidence that the maintainer works with agent-written requirements as text every day; they are not the requirements this problem is to be solved for.
- Pointing from a requirement at something outside its own document, for the initial version: asked on 2026-10-13 whether a requirement can refer to elements of other tools or to a requirement in another file, the maintainer answered "for the initial version only internal references in the current document can be made." So a requirement that points at an element of another tool (a diagram element, a knowledge row) or at a requirement in another file is not part of what is to be solved first. "For the initial version" bounds the first version and not the problem: the answer does not say that such references are never wanted (see Resolved, under Open Questions).

Requirements that concern something other than a software application are not a non-goal: asked on 2026-10-13, the maintainer answered "Nothing is out of scope by default. The tool can be used for any kind of requirement engineering" (see Resolved, under Open Questions).

## Cost of Inaction

Requirements stay as they are: Markdown that agents write and people read in an editor or a preview, flat and per feature, discussed in chat, with no one place that follows an application's structure. The nearest non-text view remains the heading outline of the standalone host's Markdown editor (research, Market & Context). For the organization's own `spec.md` and `requirements.md` files this is so whether or not anything is built, since they are out of scope (see Non-Goals); what inaction leaves as it is, is the requirements people keep elsewhere.

What kind of cost that is, is on record; how large it is, is not. Asked on 2026-10-13, the maintainer named time: a person who has to skim through text and can only search it as text is "very slow" at finding what they look for and at seeing how it all fits together (see Resolved, under Open Questions). So what inaction keeps is that slowness, each time a person looks something up in the requirements or judges them. The maintainer copes with this way of working every day, across 59 specifications in two repositories, and nothing on record says how much time it takes, how often, or whether it also leads to mistakes; the research marks the cost as an assumption with low confidence, and the answer names the cost without measuring it. No issue in the organization asks for a change, no other user is waiting, and no deadline is attached, so nothing breaks and no one is let down if this is never built.

Inaction also has a gain that the decision has to weigh. The standalone host's catalogue holds 98 tool types, of which 64 are only identified and one designer exists, as a prototype; the idea as captured would change four specifications for a first user who is the tool engineer (research, Data & Constraints, Evidence Against). Not building it leaves that effort for tools whose need is evidenced. The maintainer has weighed this: asked on 2026-10-13 whether the need justifies changing those four specifications, the answer was "yes it does" (see Resolved, under Open Questions). So the gain of inaction is on record as one the maintainer judges smaller than the need; the effort itself is not made smaller by the answer, and no evidence of the need was added with it.

One cost grows with time, on the maintainer's own account of why this presses now: agent based development is increasingly being used (see Resolved, under Open Questions). If the practice keeps moving towards more agent-written text that is kept after the work is done, the amount a person has to read and judge grows with it, and so does the number of people who have to (research, Market & Context; one article, confidence medium). How fast is not on record, so this says that the cost of inaction rises and not what it is at any moment; the paragraphs above stand, that nothing breaks and no one is let down on a given day.

Waiting is, for a time, what the maintainer has chosen. Asked on 2026-10-13 how this sequences with the open work on the standalone host, the maintainer answered "After that work" (see Resolved, under Open Questions). So the cost of inaction is carried at least until that work is done, on the maintainer's own decision, and what `/speckit-assess-decide` weighs is no longer acting now against never acting but acting after that work against never acting. What the wait itself costs is the slowness above over the time that work takes; neither the one nor the other has a figure on record.

Resolved, and kept here so the later stages see the answer:

- **Which file interaction behaviour applies.** Asked on 2026-10-13, the maintainer answered "check how this is done in the FBL." So the behaviour is FBL's, which is also what stores a designer's document (DED builds on FBL for storage), and it is read here from `specifications/fbl/FBL-specification.md`:
  - *Which part of the file changed* means no more than the standalone host does today. A host watches every open body; when it changes from outside, "the host reads it again, applies the difference to every open reading (elements matched by id keep their view data), and clears the body's history" (section 7.3). The body is read again whole and the difference is taken on the elements, by id, not on lines or bytes; a plugin-read body is treated the same, the host calling `read` again (section 11.3). With unsaved edits in the host, the user is told and chooses between reloading and keeping the editor's version (section 7.3). FBL does not merge concurrent edits (section 1.5).
  - *What is shown while the file is in error* depends on when the error is found, and both of the behaviours the question named are FBL's. A file that is open and is found unreadable on a reload "keeps the last readable model on screen, read-only, with the finding" (section 7.3). A file that is unreadable when it is opened has no last readable model, and "opens as an empty, read-only model with one finding", `std.unparseable`, located at the problem; it is never written (section 7.5).
  - *A file that is only partly wrong is not in error.* "Reading MUST NOT fail on the content of a body": an entry that cannot be read is reported at its place in the file, its bytes are kept untouched, and the other entries are read and stay editable (section 7.4). The intake's "when it is not possible to update the designer then an error is shown" is therefore read as the unreadable body of section 7.5, and a single bullet the designer cannot follow as a finding on that bullet.
  - What this leaves open is which faults in the Markdown make the body unreadable as a whole and which are a finding on one bullet. FBL leaves that to the reading of the format (section 7.5, "when a plugin reports it unreadable"), so it belongs with the question above on how the file is written.
  - The research found that the Knowledge designer's code shows an empty, read-only table also after a reload, where section 7.3 keeps the last readable content (research, The file interaction patterns of the standalone host). The answer names FBL, so FBL's behaviour is the one taken here, not that code's.
- **The structure a hierarchy starts with.** Asked on 2026-10-13 which levels, how deep, with or without requirements, whether a document can start empty, and what an application that is not a web application starts with, the maintainer answered "Max two levels. Minimal requirements per level (max 3). The document is filled when created by the tool." The answer has three sentences and settles three of the five parts:
  - *How deep*: the structure a hierarchy starts with is at most two levels deep. This is read as a limit on the starting structure only, since that is what the question asked about, and the intake's answer that the hierarchy itself has no maximum depth stands: a user or an agent can nest deeper afterwards.
  - *With or without requirements*: with them, and few. Each level of the starting structure comes with a minimal set of requirements, three at the most. "Per level" is read here as per hierarchy level in the starting structure, not per depth; the answer does not say this in so many words, nor whether a level may come with none.
  - *Whether a document can start empty*: not when the tool creates it. A document the tool creates is filled with the starting structure. The answer speaks of creation by the tool only, so a file that an agent or a user writes as text is not covered by it.
  - Not answered: which levels the starting structure holds, and what an application that is not a web application starts with. Both stay open above.
  - This also gives the first size on record, that of a new document (two levels, at most three requirements on each). It says nothing about how large a document grows, which stays open above.

  - *It is a position, not evidence.* The research marks as untested whether a hierarchy and filters make the same content easier to judge, and the answer adds no trial, measurement or example. That question stays open above, narrowed to the evidence.
  - Not answered: what "presented correctly" comprises. The answer does not say which presentation counts as correct, or how it would be seen that a presentation is. The first is for `/speckit-assess-shape`; the second belongs with the open success metrics.
  - "Out of scope" is read as a limit on this problem only: the answer does not say that the amount of agent-written text is no problem at all, or that it may not be taken up elsewhere.
